## 1. Angle (read this first)
**The story:** In 711 the Arabs took Sindh. Within ~100 years the same Caliphate had reached Spain and Central Asia, yet it **never got past Sindh into the Indian heartland**. One of the main reasons was the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty, which ruled for about 300 years (c. 730–1036).

**Keep it accurate, because that's what makes it credible:**
- They ruled for ~300 years. The *Arab* threat was strongest in the 8th–9th centuries. The dynasty finally fell to **Mahmud of Ghazni (a Turk, 1018)**, not to the Arabs. So don't say "they stopped the Arabs for 300 years" as a flat fact. Say *"300 saal ka samrajya, jisne Arabon ko Sindh se aage nahi badhne diya."*
- **Others share the credit**: Bappa Rawal (Mewar), the Chalukya Pulakeshi Avanijanashraya (Navsari, c. 739), Lalitaditya (Kashmir). Mention them. It's true, and it stops the Rajput / other communities' comment war before it starts.
- **The Gurjar-vs-Rajput origin debate is live.** There were statue controversies in Dadri (2021) and Kaithal (2023). Present the evidence (below) and say plainly that historians debate it. The channel wins trust by being fair, not by claiming everything.

## 4. Draft script (Hindi/Hinglish, about 1,500 words ≈ 10–11 min). Rewrite it in your own voice.

**[0:00 HOOK]**
सन 711। अरब सेना सिंध जीत चुकी है। उसी दौर में, इसी ख़िलाफ़त की फ़ौजें स्पेन जीत चुकी हैं, ईरान ख़त्म हो चुका है, सेंट्रल एशिया तक झंडा गड़ चुका है। नक्शा देखिए: पश्चिम में फ़्रांस की सरहद से पूरब में सिंध तक। लेकिन सिंध के बाद… ये सैलाब रुक गया। अगले 300 साल तक अरब भारत के दिल तक नहीं पहुँच पाए। क्यों? किसने रोका? ये कहानी है एक ऐसे साम्राज्य की जिसे हमारी किताबों ने लगभग भुला दिया: **गुर्जर-प्रतिहार।**

**[0:40 STAKES: the Arab wave]**
- In 711–712, Muhammad bin Qasim conquered Sindh under the Umayyad Caliphate.
- Around 720s–730s, the Sindh governor **Junayd** sent raids eastward into Marwar, Malwa, Gujarat and Ujjain (al-Baladhuri lists them).
- Line: *"सिंध अब दरवाज़ा था, और अगला निशाना था पूरा उत्तर भारत।"*

**[1:40 NAGABHATA I: the first wall]**
- Ujjain/Avanti, c. 730–760. Nagabhata I.
- The **Gwalior inscription** of his descendant Mihir Bhoj says Nagabhata crushed the armies of a powerful **"mlechchha" king**. Historians identify these as the Arab armies from Sindh.
- At the same time, Bappa Rawal and the Chalukya Pulakeshi (Navsari plates, c. 739) also beat the Arabs back. *"ये अकेले की लड़ाई नहीं थी, पर नागभट्ट ने इसके बाद जो खड़ा किया, वो सबसे बड़ा था।"*
- The Arabs pulled back west of the Indus. They even had to build new garrison towns (al-Mahfuza) for safety.

**[3:00 THE NAME: why "Gurjara-Pratihara"?]**
- "Pratihara" means doorkeeper/guard. Their own legend links them to Lakshman, Ram's "pratihari" (Sagar-Tal / Gwalior inscriptions).
- Neighbouring kingdoms and Arab writers called them **Gurjara**. The Arab merchant Sulaiman calls the kingdom **"Juzr"**. The Rajor inscription (10th c.) says "Gurjara-Pratihara-anvaya".
- **The debate:** some historians read "Gurjara" as the tribe/community, others as the region (Gurjaratra). *"इस पर बहस आज भी है, और हम दोनों पक्ष रखेंगे। पर एक बात पर कोई बहस नहीं: इस साम्राज्य ने भारत का इतिहास बदला।"*

**[4:00 KANNAUJ: the tripartite struggle]**
- Kannauj, the "Rome of North India", was the symbol of imperial power.
- Three powers fought over it for ~200 years: the **Palas (Bengal)**, the **Rashtrakutas (Deccan)**, and the **Pratiharas**. Show it as an animated triangle map.
- **Nagabhata II** (800–833) took Kannauj and made it the capital. He also checked Arab advances again in the west (and is linked to rebuilding Somnath, per the dynasty article; verify before you say it).

**[5:30 MIHIR BHOJ: the peak]**
- Mihir Bhoj (836–885). The empire ran from the **Sutlej to Bengal, the Himalaya to the Narmada**. Show the map.
- He pushed the Arabs out of **Kutch and the fort of Sindan** (c. 833–842). Sindh's Arab power broke into two small emirates, **Multan and Mansura**.
- **Sulaiman (Arab merchant, c. 851):** *"इस राजा के पास बहुत बड़ी फ़ौज है, और भारत के किसी राजा के पास इतनी अच्छी घुड़सवार सेना नहीं… अरबों का इससे बड़ा दुश्मन कोई नहीं।"* (Show the translated line on screen, citing Elliot & Dowson Vol. 1.)
- His **"Adivaraha" coins** (Vishnu's boar avatar) circulated across North India. Show a coin image (free licence only).

**[7:00 THE MULTAN TRICK: twist]**
- Multan had a famous **Sun Temple**, a huge pilgrimage and money centre.
- Arab geographers (al-Istakhri, al-Masudi) wrote that Multan's Muslim rulers used the temple as **protection against invasion by neighbouring Hindu kings**. Pilgrims' offerings were the state's biggest income, and the threat to the idol kept attackers away.
- Line: *"सोचिए, अरब इतने डरे हुए थे कि एक मंदिर को ढाल बना लिया।"* ⚠️ The sources say "neighbouring Hindu powers". That the Pratiharas were the main such power is an inference, so say "सबसे बड़ी हिंदू ताकत प्रतिहार ही थे" as context, not as a quote.

**[8:00 ART & TEMPLES]**
- Osian (783 CE, the oldest surviving Jain temple in western India), the **Bateshwar temples** (Morena), **Teli ka Mandir** (Gwalior). Mention Bateshwar's restoration story (K.K. Muhammed) only if you verify it.

**[8:45 FALL]**
- 916: Rashtrakuta Indra III raids and captures Kannauj. The Pratiharas take it back, but feudatories break away (Chandelas, Paramaras, Kalachuris).
- **1018: Mahmud of Ghazni** reaches Kannauj, and Rajyapala flees. The Chandelas punish him for it. The dynasty ends c. 1036.
- *"जिस साम्राज्य को अरब नहीं तोड़ पाए, वो अंदर की लड़ाइयों से टूटा।"* (That's the lesson.)

**[9:45 TODAY: why it's forgotten + the statue debate]**
- Textbooks give the Pratiharas a few lines. Today they come up mostly in caste debates: the Mihir Bhoj statue rows (Dadri 2021, Kaithal 2023), and the Haryana committee that was set up to examine the facts.
- *"मेरा मानना है: मिहिर भोज पर गर्व हर भारतीय का हक़ है। इतिहास को बाँटने से ज़्यादा ज़रूरी है उसे जानना।"*
